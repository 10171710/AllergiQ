Early Peanut Introduction: New Guidelines for Preventing Allergies

Dr. Elena Rostova, MD October 2026 7 Min Read
Table of Contents:
  • 1. The LEAP Trial Paradigm Shift: Avoidance vs Early Exposure
  • 2. Risk Stratification: High-Risk vs Standard Infants
  • 3. Step-by-Step Feeding Protocol & Safe Infant Textures
  • 4. Key Takeaways for Parents

For nearly two decades, pediatric advice urged parents to delay feeding peanuts and other allergenic foods until children reached age three. Medical history now proves this avoidance policy contributed to a dramatic tripling of peanut allergy diagnoses. Today, global pediatric guidelines recommend early oral introduction between 4 and 6 months of age.

The LEAP Trial Paradigm Shift: Avoidance vs Early Exposure

The landmark LEAP (Learning Early About Peanut Allergy) clinical trial, led by Professor Gideon Lack, randomized over 600 high-risk infants to either complete peanut avoidance or regular consumption of peanut products starting between 4 and 11 months of age.

The findings were groundbreaking: early introduction reduced the development of peanut allergy at age five by an astonishing 81% to 86% compared to the avoidance group.

The biological mechanism is known as the dual-allergen-exposure hypothesis: when allergenic proteins touch broken skin (such as eczema plaques), the immune system is sensitized and produces allergic IgE antibodies. Conversely, when the same protein enters through the gastrointestinal tract during early infancy, mucosal gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) generates active oral immune tolerance.

Clinical Advice for Parents

"The critical window of opportunity is between 4 and 6 months of life. If an infant has severe eczema or an existing egg allergy, we recommend a rapid skin prick test or blood IgE evaluation in our pediatric suite before offering peanut at home. For all other babies, you can safely introduce smooth peanut at home as soon as they start solid foods."
Dr. Elena Rostova, MD, Pediatric Allergy Specialist

Step-by-Step Feeding Protocol & Safe Infant Textures

Step Safe Preparation Method Important Safety Precaution
1. Timing & Readiness Baby is 46 months old, can hold head steady, and has tried initial purees (carrots, sweet potato). Pick a morning when the child is healthy and can be monitored for 2 hours post-feed.
2. Texture Preparation Thin 2 teaspoons of smooth peanut butter with 23 teaspoons of warm water, breastmilk, or formula. NEVER feed whole peanuts or thick spoonfuls (choking hazard).
3. Initial Taste Offer a tiny dab on the tip of a spoon. Wait 10 minutes to observe for any immediate hives or swelling. If no reaction occurs, offer the remainder at the infant's normal eating pace.
4. Maintenance Maintain approximately 6 grams of peanut protein per week (spread across 23 feeds or Bamba puffs). Ongoing regular consumption is necessary to maintain lifelong oral tolerance.

Key Takeaways for Parents

  • Do not delay allergenic foods: Delaying peanuts, eggs, or tree nuts past 12 months increases the risk of sensitization.
  • Consistency is key: Introducing peanut once is not enough; ongoing weekly exposure throughout infancy and toddlerhood maintains tolerance.
  • Clinic-supervised oral challenges: If you are nervous or your child has moderate-to-severe eczema, schedule a supervised in-clinic oral food challenge with our pediatric team.
Medical Literature References:
  1. Lack G, et al. Randomized Trial of Peanut Consumption in Infants at Risk for Peanut Allergy (LEAP). N Engl J Med, 2015; 372:803-813.
  2. National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID): Addendum Guidelines for the Prevention of Peanut Allergy in the United States, 2024.
