Mechanism of Action

The Science Behind Sublingual Drops (SLIT)

How mucosal immune presentation creates lasting systemic protection without injections.

1

Sublingual Dendritic Capture

The mucosa beneath your tongue is rich in specialized antigen-presenting dendritic cells. Micro-dosed allergen drops bind directly to these receptors in 2 minutes.

2

T-Regulatory (Treg) Induction

Dendritic cells travel to cervical lymph nodes, signaling naive T-cells to transform into immunosuppressive Treg cells rather than inflammatory Th2 cells.

3

IgG4 & Secretory IgA Barrier

Your B-cells begin producing protective IgG4 blocking antibodies. Future airborne pollen is neutralized before it can trigger histamine release.

SLIT delivers a daily, precisely measured dose of allergen extract under the tongue, where specialized dendritic cells capture it and gradually retrain your immune system to stop overreacting. Over time your body builds regulatory T-cells, IgG4 blocking antibodies, and a mucosal barrier that calms both allergic rhinitis and mild allergic asthma.

Yes. SLIT is self-administered at home using a graduated dosing calendar. The first dose of each new concentration is taken in-clinic under observation; after that, daily doses are taken at home. Serious reactions are rare, and every plan includes a 24/7 clinical hotline and a written emergency action plan.

Many patients notice less sneezing, congestion, and eye itching within 8 to 12 weeks, with peak benefit at 12 to 24 months of consistent use. The dose tracker and streak alerts inside your patient portal make it easy to stay on schedule, which is the single biggest factor in how well SLIT works.

Missing a single day is usually fine, just resume the next day. If you miss more than a few days, message your clinician through the portal so your schedule can be adjusted. Drops are shelf-stable and travel-friendly, and members on our plan can request quarterly refills shipped anywhere in the US.
