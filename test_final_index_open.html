Seasonality Guide

The Four Seasons of Airborne Allergens

Identify when your trigger peaks throughout the calendar year and prepare your immune defense.

Spring (March - May)
Tree Pollens

Oak, Birch, Cedar, Elm, and Ash trees release fine powdery pollen that travels hundreds of miles on windy spring days.

Key Symptoms: Intense eye itching, morning sneezing fits, sinus pressure, nasal congestion.
Summer (June - August)
Grass Pollens

Timothy, Bermuda, Kentucky Bluegrass, and Orchard grasses thrive and pollinate during warm sunny afternoons.

Key Symptoms: Thunderstorm asthma flare-ups, grass contact hives, throat tickle.
Fall (Sept - November)
Weed & Molds

Ragweed plants release billions of pollen grains, while decaying autumn leaves trigger outdoor Alternaria mold spores.

Key Symptoms: Post-nasal drip, chronic cough, fatigue, night-time wheezing.
Year-Round Indoor
Dust & Dander

Dust mites in bedding, pet dander, and damp bathroom fungal spores trigger constant indoor allergies for sensitive individuals.

Key Symptoms: Year-round nasal congestion, eczema patches, waking up stuffy.
